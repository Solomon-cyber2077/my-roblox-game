# LORE: Last Train Out, lore bible and period reference

Every change to story, names, signs, clothing, props or architecture follows this file. If a request conflicts with it,
flag it rather than drift. The design source of truth for mechanics is still the blueprint linked in README.md.

## 1. Era and setting

- **Year range: 1884-1892.** Late Victorian, after the gauge conversions (Minehead's branch went from broad to standard
  gauge in 1882) and before the big Edwardian rebuilds. Gas light, steam, telegraph, oil lamps in the carriages.
  No electric street lighting outside a few big cities, no motor cars, no telephones in country stations.
- **Where:** a West Country branch line in the manner of the Minehead branch (opened 16 July 1874, run by the Bristol &
  Exeter, later Great Western). Seaside resort at one end, mining villages, farm halts and market towns along the way.
  The fictional company is the **Severn & Western Counties Railway (S&WCR)**, so we never wear a real company's livery.
- **What a journey meant:** the railway was the only fast way out. Third-class fares (a penny a mile by law) let
  labourers, maids and miners travel; first class carried the gentry in upholstered compartments. A timetable was a
  promise. Missing the last train meant a night in a strange town, a long walk in the dark, or nothing at all.
- **Why the last train matters:** one night the line keeps running while everything along it goes wrong. Stations
  empty, fog comes off the moor, the telegraph answers with nothing. The crew are whoever happened to be aboard. The
  train is the only lit, warm, moving thing left, and it will not wait. Being left on the platform is the real death.

## 2. Tone

- **Horror survival like Outlast:** players cannot kill the Hollows. They hide, run, pry each other free, close doors
  and leave on time. Fear comes from being slower, louder and fewer than what is out there.
- **Dark fantasy mood, grounded rules:** no player powers, no spells, no glowing runes, no magic system, no chosen
  ones. The uncanny lives in the world (the blood moon, the TallOnes on the horizon, omens), never in the players' hands.
- **Taste reference: Guts and Black Powder.** Feeling is carried by clothing, place names, worn materials and light,
  not by exposition. Show a soot-black waiting room and a stopped clock; do not explain them.
- **Dignity of the period:** slightly elegant clothes, good lettering, polished brass. The horror is that this ordered
  world is coming apart. Keep humour dry and rare (a ledger note, a misprinted poster), never modern or meme-like.

## 3. The Hollows (kept vague)

- People call them Hollows because of how they look from a distance: hollow in the face, hollow in the walk.
  That is all anyone knows. Notes and posters may contradict each other; nothing in game states the truth.
- **Do not commit to a twist.** No "they were the passengers", no "the train is a ferry for the dead", no
  purgatory, no cult, no experiment, no single cause. Leave the question open; let players argue about it.
- Allowed hints: missing-person notices, a stationmaster's last log entry, Sunday sermon notices about "the Thinning",
  a child's chalk drawing, timetables with stations crossed out. Each hint should suggest, never confirm.
- They come with fog and dark, they avoid lamplight less than people hope, and they want to hold on.

## 4. Names and places

**Conventions**
- Stations: a real-sounding English place name, sometimes with a suffix for clarity: `Halt` (unstaffed stop),
  `Road` (station miles from its village), `Junction`, `Sands`/`on-Sea` (resort), `Town` vs `Parish`.
- Place names use West Country and Old English roots: -combe, -cott, -ford, -stow, -worthy, -ton, -leigh, -hay,
  Nether/Upper/Kings/Bishops prefixes, saints' names (St. Decuman's).
- Streets: Station Road, Church Lane, Fore Street, High Street, Quay Street, Mill Leat, Tanners' Row, Chapel Steps.
- Shops: surname + trade, often "& Son" or "& Co.": "J. Hobley & Son, Ironmongers", "Mrs. Pyle, Drapery & Millinery".
- School: "<Village> National School" (Church) or "<Village> Board School" (after 1870), with a date stone.
- Church: "St. <Saint>'s" parish church; a plain "Wesleyan Chapel" or "Bible Christian Chapel" (strong in Devon).
- Pubs: "The <Animal/Trade> Inn": The Plough, The Railway Hotel, The Blue Anchor, The Lamb & Flag.

**Usable names (none tied to a real company)**
- Stations: Ashcombe Halt, Wyvern Sands, Nether Stowey Road, Kingsfold Junction, Brendon Cross, Halsewood,
  Old Cleave, Withiel Parish, Exmoor Gate, Bishops Lydiard Halt, Culverhay, St. Decuman's, Coldharbour Siding,
  Treborough Mines, Monksilver.
- Shops and businesses: J. Hobley & Son Ironmongers; Pyle's Drapery & Millinery; Tozer Bros. Coal & Coke Merchants;
  W. Venn, Family Butcher; The Wyvern Sands Tea Rooms; Chidgey's Temperance Hotel; Lethbridge, Chemist & Druggist;
  Gollop & Co. Corn Factors.
- School and church: Halsewood National School (1871); St. Michael's, Brendon Cross; Old Cleave Wesleyan Chapel.
- Streets: Fore Street, Tanners' Row, Quay Street, Chapel Steps, Mill Leat.

## 5. Signage and typography

- **Lettering:** bold sans-serif capitals ("grotesque", e.g. Victorian railway block letters) and condensed
  slab/Egyptian faces for posters; Clarendon-style serifs for notices; copperplate script only for private notes,
  ledgers and tickets' fine print. Roblox fonts that pass: `Oswald` (condensed grotesque), `Bodoni`/`Garamond`
  (notices), `Merriweather` (body), `Fondamento` sparingly. Fail: Gotham, SourceSans, Arial-look UI on world signs,
  `Arcade`, `Cartoon`, anything rounded or geometric-modern.
- **Station name boards:** early boards are hand-painted (cream or white letters on dark ground, shaded); later ones
  are vitreous enamel, usually **white on dark blue**, in a timber frame; some are cast-iron or screwed-on cast letters
  on a wooden board. Running-in boards stand at platform ends and name junction connections.
- **Cast-iron notices:** raised letters, painted (e.g. "NOTICE. ANY PERSON TRESPASSING..."), dated, signed
  "By Order".
- **Posters and timetables:** letterpress, black ink on buff/cream paper, many typefaces mixed on one sheet, centred,
  rules and borders, small woodcut engravings. Pasted on boards, overlapping, torn, damp-stained.
- **Palette:** Brunswick green, chocolate and cream (stock and buildings), Indian red, deep ultramarine (enamel),
  black, buff, brass/gilt for lettering on company property. Signs are faded, sooted and chipped, never neon-clean.
- **Materials:** enamel on steel, painted timber, cast iron, gilt transfers on glass, brass plates, chalk on blackboard.

## 6. Clothing (only what existed 1884-1892)

- **Working-class men:** collarless or banded shirts, waistcoat, corduroy or moleskin trousers, braces, hobnail boots,
  flat cap or bowler, neckerchief, fustian jacket, oilskin for weather, leather apron for trades.
- **Working-class women:** plain wool or cotton bodice and skirt, apron or pinafore, shawl, straw or felt bonnet,
  button boots; maids in print dresses with white cap and apron.
- **Railway staff:** frock or reefer coat in dark blue/black with brass buttons, peaked cap with company badge,
  waistcoat with watch chain; porters in sleeved waistcoats and corduroy; guards with whistle, flags and oil lamp;
  stationmaster in frock coat and top hat or peaked cap with gold braid; drivers/firemen in blue cotton jackets and
  caps, soot-stained.
- **Shopkeepers:** sack suits, waistcoats, celluloid or detachable collars, sleeve garters, aprons, bowlers;
  women in tailored bodices with bustles (mid-1880s) or the slimmer early-1890s line, lace collars.
- **Gentry (elegant options):** men in frock coats, morning coats, Inverness capes, ulsters, top hats, kid gloves,
  walking canes, pocket watches; women in bustled walking dresses (to c.1889), then leg-o'-mutton sleeves (from c.1891),
  fitted jackets, mantles, fur tippets, veiled hats, parasols, muffs.
- **Never:** zips, wristwatches, trench coats, trilbies/fedoras, sunglasses, trainers, hoodies, jeans with modern cut,
  shorts on adults, plastic, bright synthetic colours.

## 7. Props and architecture

- **Lighting:** gas lamps on cast-iron fluted posts with square glazed lanterns (station name often on the glass);
  oil hand lamps, carriage roof lamps, candle lanterns, paraffin table lamps. Warm yellow-orange light only.
- **Luggage:** domed and flat-top steamer trunks with wooden slats and brass corners, leather portmanteaus, Gladstone
  bags, hat boxes, carpet bags, wicker hampers, milk churns, parcels in brown paper and string, porters' barrows.
- **Rooms:** waiting rooms with fireplaces, wall clocks, benches with cast-iron ends (often a company monogram),
  bookshelves of cloth-bound books, ticket windows with brass grilles, Edmondson card tickets, ticket racks, ledgers,
  telegraph instruments, notice boards, leather fire buckets.
- **Tea room:** marble-topped counter, glass bell cake stands, brown Betty and silver-plate teapots, china cups with
  saucers, urn, sandwiches under glass, Bath buns, enamel advertisements for tea and cocoa.
- **Canopies:** cast-iron columns with decorative brackets, timber valance boards with sawtooth or "dagger" edge,
  glazed or slate ridge roofs, often painted cream and chocolate or green; small halts have a timber shelter only.
- **Buildings:** local stone or red brick with dressed quoins, slate roofs, tall chimneys, sash windows, Gothic chapels,
  board schools with bell turrets, shopfronts with fascia boards and gilt lettering, cobbles and setts, iron railings.

## 8. Period checklist (run before finishing any change)

1. Nothing invented after 1892 (no electric street lights, cars, phones, zips, plastics, wristwatches).
2. World text uses an approved period font; no modern UI faces on signs, posters or props.
3. Colours come from the palette in section 5; no neon, no clean saturated modern tones.
4. Materials read as wood, iron, brick, stone, brass, enamel, glass, paper, wool, leather.
5. Names follow section 4 and do not use a real railway company's name or livery.
6. Clothing items appear in section 6 (or are clearly of the same period).
7. No magic, no player powers, no way to kill a Hollow.
8. Hollow lore stays open: no explanation, no ferry/afterlife twist.
9. Signs and posters look used: faded, sooted, chipped or torn somewhere.
